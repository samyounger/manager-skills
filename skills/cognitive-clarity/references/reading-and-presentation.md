# Reading and presentation

These are adaptable communication practices, not a treatment or a claim that everyone with dyslexia has the same needs. The user's stated preferences take priority.

## Summarising a long source

Start with the source's main message and what it means for the user's task. Follow with up to three key points, then decisions or actions if present.

Preserve qualifications that change the interpretation. Attribute claims to the source when they are not independently verified. Do not silently merge conflicting sources or turn a summary into an endorsement. Keep links close to the claims they support.

When the user requests a full analysis, include it after the short overview. Avoid reproducing the source's order when a decision-focused order is clearer.

## Explaining or teaching

Start with a plain-English explanation and one concrete example. Introduce necessary terminology after its purpose is clear. For a complex process, use a few meaningful stages before expanding individual stages.

Offer alternative representations when useful: a short example, a small diagram, or a compact comparison. Do not automatically generate all three. A visual should reduce reading effort and have a short text explanation.

## Reduce effort within sentences

Use these practices when editing explanations, proposals, and updates:

- Unpack noun stacks into explicit relationships. For example, change “customer feedback triage workflow optimisation” to “improve how we sort customer feedback and decide what to act on”.
- Keep the subject and its verb close together. Move long qualifications into another sentence when that makes the connection clearer.
- Keep helpful connecting words. Do not routinely remove “that”, “which”, “because”, or “so” just to shorten a sentence. Split embedded clauses when readers would otherwise have to hold several ideas in memory.
- Prefer familiar phrases and concrete verbs. Replace an invented verb or a metaphor when interpreting it adds effort. Preserve established technical terms when they help the intended audience.
- Check the reading burden of the whole answer. Remove repetition, but retain the context, examples, and causal links needed to understand it. A string of short fragments can still be difficult to follow.

These are editing judgements, not blanket bans or fixed sentence-length targets.

## Evidence and its limits

[Clark, Martin and Mahowald (2026), *Why is AI-generated language so hard to understand?*](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29162v1) found that many models overused linguistic features associated with human processing difficulty. A separate experiment showed that penalising response length could shift a small model towards shorter grammatical forms, with some transfer to untrained constructions.

The practices above are an application of those findings, not a remedy validated by the study. It measured linguistic indicators, not reader comprehension, and did not specifically study dyslexia. Its English-only data and limited conversation seeds restrict generalisation. It does not establish the cause of commercial models' writing patterns or show that every short form, metaphor, or noun compound is difficult.

## Instructions

Use numbered steps with one clear action per step. Keep commands in separate code blocks. Place a necessary caution immediately before the affected action. State the expected result or check when needed.

Give the whole procedure when requested. Use one step at a time when the user requests guided interaction or later steps depend on observations or approval. Do not add a confirmation after every routine step.

## Text intended for listening

When requested, produce a version with short sentences and explicit transitions. Expand acronyms, describe essential symbols, and express table comparisons in prose. Keep exact commands or identifiers separately for copying; do not alter their executable spelling to make them sound better.

Do not claim a read-aloud feature exists in a particular application without verifying it. Do not create audio or change accessibility settings unless requested.

## Documents where layout is controllable

Prefer a clear heading hierarchy, left-aligned body text, manageable line lengths and visible paragraph spacing. Avoid fully justified body text, long italic passages, all-capital paragraphs and colour as the only signal.

Respect the user's chosen font, size, contrast and background. Treat alternatives as preferences to try, not universally effective prescriptions. Do not claim that a specialist font, colour or overlay is proven to help everyone with dyslexia.

Keep tables small enough to scan. Explain the decision in prose rather than making the reader reconstruct it from cells. Follow the relevant document or presentation workflow to verify the actual rendered layout; Markdown instructions alone cannot guarantee font, spacing or accessibility in the destination application.
