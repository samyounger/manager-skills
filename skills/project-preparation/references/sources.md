# Senior Engineering Management Project Planning — Sources & Further Reading

Use this reference when an epic is part of a multi-team initiative, a portfolio decision, a platform investment, or an AI-enabled delivery change. It is deliberately not a universal checklist for ordinary team-level work.

## Current Planning Lenses

### Value, not merely scope, schedule, and budget

The 2025 PMI *Pulse of the Profession* describes business acumen as the ability to connect delivery to strategy, finance, industry context, and stakeholder needs. For a senior engineering manager, translate this into a portfolio practice: make the intended value, material trade-offs, and reason for priority visible alongside delivery status. A project that is on time but no longer advances the strategy should be reconsidered.

- [PMI Pulse of the Profession 2025: Boosting Business Acumen](https://www.pmi.org/learning/thought-leadership/boosting-business-acumen)

### Manage the conditions for delivery, not just activity

Google Cloud's 2025 DORA research finds that AI can improve individual productivity and product performance while also exposing weaknesses in delivery stability. It also reports a strong relationship between quality internal platforms and an organisation's ability to benefit from AI. The senior-EM implication is to make the operating conditions visible in planning: workflow clarity, feedback loops, platform constraints, adoption risks, and whether increased change volume can be absorbed safely.

- [2025 DORA report: State of AI-assisted software development](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report)
- [2025 DORA AI Capabilities Model report](https://cloud.google.com/resources/content/2025-dora-ai-capabilities-model-report)

### Treat complexity as a coordination and decision-rights problem

PMI's 2026 *Pulse of the Profession* reports that complex work is increasingly shaped by strategic drift, delivery disruption, and team strain when coordination and decision rights are unclear. Use that as a planning prompt: identify the decisions that need an owner, the few dependencies that could change the plan, and the escalation path. Do not respond by making every risk or dependency a senior-management concern.

- [PMI Pulse of the Profession 2026: Driving Success in Complex Projects](https://www.pmi.org/about/press-media/2026/pulse-why-complex-projects-fail-best-practices-are-not-enough-system-thinking)

## Applying These Lenses to an Epic

For a portfolio-level epic, use Portfolio Status to answer five questions succinctly:

1. **Value:** What customer, business, risk-reduction, or capability outcome makes this work worth doing now?
2. **Decision:** What material scope, sequence, or investment decision is next, and who owns it?
3. **Dependencies:** Which external team, artifact, or decision could change the delivery path?
4. **System health:** Is there a meaningful delivery, platform, workflow, reliability, or AI-adoption condition that changes confidence?
5. **Management action:** Does the engineering manager need to decide, escalate, unblock a dependency, or make a scope call?

Keep the answer proportionate. A single-team, low-risk epic may need only a short status and no senior-level action. Use the fuller framing when it changes prioritisation, staffing, sequencing, or delivery confidence.
